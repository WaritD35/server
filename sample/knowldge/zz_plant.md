# ZZ plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia) — visual care reference

## Healthy appearance

A healthy ZZ plant displays glossy, dark green oval leaves arranged symmetrically along thick, fleshy stalks. The stalks of a healthy ZZ plant stand upright or gently arch outward from the center of the pot. The base of each healthy green stalk appears swollen and structurally firm right where the stalk meets the potting soil. A beginner might mistake the pale, bright green color of newly emerging stalks for a nutrient deficiency. The bright pale green color of newly emerging stalks is completely normal, and the new leaves will gradually darken to match the older foliage over several weeks. A beginner might also mistake the slow shedding of a single older leaf at the very bottom of the plant for a watering problem. The gradual yellowing and dropping of a single very old leaf near the base is a natural part of the plant aging process. The visible surface of the potting soil inside the container typically appears completely dry, light-colored, and free of visible mold or thick white mineral crusts.

## Problems

### Bright Yellowing and Collapsing Stalks

* Care need: overwatering
* Visible signs: Entire stalks turn a bright, solid yellow color and droop heavily over the side of the pot. The base of the yellowing stalks near the soil line looks mushy, translucent, or physically collapsed.
* Healthy contrast: The stalks of a healthy ZZ plant maintain a dark green color and a rigid, firm structure directly at the soil line.
* Not to be confused with: Bright yellowing and collapsing stalks should not be confused with the pale green color of brand new growth. Brand new growth stands rigidly upright and appears light green, whereas overwatering causes mature stalks to turn bright yellow and physically collapse at the base.
* Likely cause: The potting soil remains constantly wet, causing the thick underground rhizomes and lower stalk bases to rot.
* Action: Stop watering the plant immediately until the entire visible surface of the potting soil appears completely dry and dusty. Use clean scissors to cut the mushy, collapsed yellow stalks completely off at the soil line.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The collapsed yellow stalks will never recover, but the rotting process will stop spreading to the remaining green stalks, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.

### Deeply Wrinkled Stalks and Shriveling Leaves

* Care need: water_deficit
* Visible signs: Deep, vertical wrinkles appear running up and down the entire length of the main green stalks. The normally plump, oval leaves look slightly deflated, lose the glossy shine, and begin to curl slightly inward along the edges.
* Healthy contrast: The stalks of a well-hydrated ZZ plant look entirely smooth and plump, while the leaves appear completely flat and highly glossy.
* Not to be confused with: Deeply wrinkled stalks should not be confused with collapsing yellow stalks caused by overwatering. Overwatering causes the stalk base to turn mushy and rot away, whereas a water deficit causes the entire green stalk to shrink and wrinkle while remaining physically intact and firmly attached to the base.
* Likely cause: The potting soil has remained completely bone dry for several months, fully depleting the stored water reserves inside the plant.
* Action: Pour room-temperature water evenly over the top of the potting soil until a steady stream flows out of the bottom drainage holes of the pot. Empty the saucer beneath the plant pot thirty minutes after the initial watering to prevent the plant from sitting in stagnant water.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The vertical wrinkles on the main stalks will smooth out and the leaves will regain a plump, glossy appearance, typically within 3 days, at most 7 days.

### Extreme Leaning and Widely Spaced Leaves

* Care need: low_light
* Visible signs: New stalks grow unusually long, thin, and bend dramatically outward toward the nearest window. The individual oval leaves growing on the exceptionally long stalks are much smaller than older leaves and have large vertical gaps between each leaflet.
* Healthy contrast: A healthy ZZ plant produces thick, sturdy stalks that grow mostly upright with large leaflets clustered closely together.
* Not to be confused with: Extreme leaning and widely spaced leaves should not be confused with the mushy collapse caused by overwatering. A mushy collapse happens exactly at the soil line due to rotting, whereas low light causes structurally sound but overly thin stalks to bend gradually under their own weight.
* Likely cause: The plant is located in a room completely devoid of natural light and is stretching weakly to find a light source.
* Action: Move the plant pot to a brighter location that receives ambient, indirect sunlight. Ensure the new location is bright enough for a human to easily read a printed book without turning on an overhead electric light.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The currently stretched and leaning stalks will remain permanently bent, but brand new stalks emerging from the soil will grow thick, compact, and strictly upright, typically within 4 weeks, at most 12 weeks.

### Faded Pale Leaves with Dry Brown Scorch Marks

* Care need: excess_light
* Visible signs: The uppermost leaves directly facing a window lose the characteristic dark green color and fade to a washed-out, pale yellow-white. Large, dry, papery brown patches develop directly in the center of the faded leaves on the side of the plant closest to the light source.
* Healthy contrast: The uppermost leaves of a healthy ZZ plant maintain a uniform, rich dark green color without any dry, papery patches.
* Not to be confused with: Faded pale leaves with dry brown scorch marks should not be confused with the overall yellowing caused by overwatering. Overwatering turns the entire lower stalk bright yellow, whereas excess light physically bleaches and burns exclusively the top leaves directly exposed to intense sun.
* Likely cause: Direct, harsh afternoon sunlight is striking the plant and physically burning the leaf tissue.
* Action: Move the plant pot several feet away from the intensely sunny window. Position the plant pot in a spot where the incoming natural daylight casts a soft, blurry shadow rather than a harsh, sharply defined shadow.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The burned brown patches and severely bleached leaves will never turn green again, but the plant will stop developing new scorch marks on healthy foliage, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.

### Black Water-Soaked Patches on Leaves

* Care need: temperature_stress
* Visible signs: Broad sections of the oval leaves suddenly turn a very dark black color and take on a visibly wet, mushy appearance. The black mushy damage appears abruptly, usually on the outer foliage physically closest to a drafty window pane or an exterior door.
* Healthy contrast: A healthy ZZ plant features entirely green, glossy foliage that remains structurally firm and visually dry to the eye.
* Not to be confused with: Black water-soaked patches on leaves should not be confused with dry brown scorch marks caused by excess light. Dry brown scorch marks look physically dry and crispy to the eye, whereas temperature stress causes broad sections of the leaf to turn black and appear visibly wet and limp.
* Likely cause: The plant has been exposed to freezing temperatures, a severe cold draft, or direct physical contact with a freezing glass windowpane.
* Action: Relocate the plant pot to a warm, stable indoor room far away from drafty windows, exterior doors, and active air conditioning vents. Use clean household scissors to cut off the visibly black and mushy leaves.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The removed black sections cannot be healed, but the remaining healthy green foliage will stop developing new mushy black patches, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.

### Small Fluffy White Masses on Stems

* Care need: pest
* Visible signs: Tiny, fluffy white blobs that resemble small tufts of cotton appear wedged tightly into the crevices where the individual leaflets attach to the main green stalk. The leaves immediately below the fluffy white masses may look uncharacteristically shiny and covered in a sticky residue.
* Healthy contrast: The crevices where the leaflets attach to the main stalk on a healthy ZZ plant are completely clean, smooth, and free of any white fuzz or sticky residue.
* Not to be confused with: Small fluffy white masses on stems should not be confused with white mineral crusts on the potting soil. White mineral crusts lie flat directly on the dirt surface, whereas the pest masses are fluffy and stick directly to the upper plant stems and leaf joints.
* Likely cause: Mealybugs have infested the plant and are hiding in the protected stem crevices to feed on the internal plant sap.
* Action: Wipe the visible fluffy white masses off the stems and leaves using a cotton swab heavily soaked in standard rubbing alcohol. Spray the entire visible plant canopy lightly with a commercial insecticidal soap designed specifically for indoor houseplants.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The visible white cotton-like masses will disappear immediately upon wiping, and no new fluffy white patches will reappear in the stem crevices, typically within 1 week, at most 3 weeks.
