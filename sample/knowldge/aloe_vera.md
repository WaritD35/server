# Aloe vera (Aloe barbadensis miller) — visual care reference

## Healthy appearance

A healthy Aloe vera displays thick, fleshy, spear-shaped leaves that grow upward and outward from a tight central rosette at the soil line. The leaves of a healthy Aloe vera feature a muted greyish-green color and have small, pale, flexible teeth running continuously along the entire outer edge of each leaf. A healthy plant holds the heavy leaves firmly upright or at a slight outward angle without bending or creasing. A beginner might mistake the presence of small, pale white spots scattered across newly emerging leaves for a pest infestation or a disease. The small pale white spots on young foliage are a completely normal genetic feature of the plant, and the white spots typically fade away naturally as the individual leaves mature and darken. A beginner might also mistake the sudden appearance of miniature Aloe plants pushing up through the soil around the main base for a weed problem. The miniature plants at the base are perfectly normal offshoots called pups, which demonstrate that the main plant is thriving and reproducing. A beginner might worry about a single leaf at the very bottom of the rosette turning completely dry, brown, and papery. The occasional drying out of a single lowest leaf is a harmless, natural part of the Aloe vera life cycle as the plant sheds old growth. The visible surface of the potting soil generally appears coarse, sandy, highly porous, and visually dry without any green moss or fluffy mold.

## Problems

### Mushy Yellowing at the Base

* Care need: overwatering
* Visible signs: The oldest leaves located at the very bottom of the rosette turn a pale, sickly yellow and become visibly translucent or mushy directly at the soil line. The mushy yellow leaves completely lose structural strength, collapsing flat against the rim of the pot or drooping heavily onto the potting soil.
* Healthy contrast: The lowest outer leaves of a healthy Aloe vera maintain firm structural integrity at the soil line and easily support the heavy weight of the fleshy foliage.
* Not to be confused with: Mushy yellowing at the base should not be confused with the natural shedding of an old leaf. The natural shedding of an old leaf results in a leaf that looks physically dry, brown, and papery, whereas overwatering causes several lower leaves to simultaneously rot, turn intensely yellow, and look visibly wet and mushy.
* Likely cause: The potting soil remains constantly wet and soggy for extended periods, suffocating the roots and causing the fleshy plant base to rot.
* Action: Stop adding water to the Aloe vera immediately. Wait to water again until the visible top surface of the potting soil appears significantly lighter in color and looks completely bone dry to the eye.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The collapsed mushy leaves will permanently die and fall off, but the rapid rotting process will stop spreading to the remaining healthy central leaves, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.

### Concave and Wrinkled Leaves

* Care need: water_deficit
* Visible signs: The normally plump leaves become noticeably thin and curl inward along the edges to form a hollow U-shape or a concave trough. Deep, vertical wrinkles appear running lengthwise up and down the entire flat surface of the green leaves, and the sharply pointed leaf tips may turn dry and brown.
* Healthy contrast: The leaves of a well-hydrated Aloe vera appear uniformly thick, plump, and smooth, displaying a slightly convex or completely flat upper surface without any deep vertical wrinkles.
* Not to be confused with: Concave and wrinkled leaves should not be confused with dark sunken disease spots. Disease spots appear as isolated, circular craters on an otherwise plump leaf, whereas a water deficit causes the entire leaf body to physically shrink, fold inward, and look severely dehydrated across the entire plant.
* Likely cause: The potting soil has remained completely bone dry for a prolonged period, forcing the plant to fully deplete the water reserves stored inside the fleshy leaves.
* Action: Pour room-temperature water evenly over the entire top surface of the potting soil until a steady stream of water exits the bottom drainage holes of the pot. Empty the saucer beneath the plant pot thirty minutes after the initial watering to prevent the pot from sitting in stagnant water.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The vertical wrinkles will slowly plump up and smooth out, and the concave leaves will expand to become flat and thick again, typically within 3 days, at most 7 days.

### Reddish-Brown Discoloration

* Care need: excess_light
* Visible signs: The broad surfaces of the leaves change from the normal greyish-green color to a striking bronze, red, or light brown shade. The reddish-brown discoloration affects the entire plant canopy or is heavily concentrated entirely on the side of the plant directly facing the nearest window.
* Healthy contrast: A healthy Aloe vera maintains a uniform, muted greyish-green color across all leaves, regardless of which direction the leaves face.
* Not to be confused with: Reddish-brown discoloration should not be confused with the mushy yellowing caused by overwatering. Overwatered leaves become soft, physically collapse, and turn pale yellow, whereas leaves experiencing reddish-brown discoloration remain completely firm, plump, and rigidly upright.
* Likely cause: Direct, harsh, intense sunlight is hitting the plant and triggering a protective color change in the plant tissue, commonly referred to as sun stress.
* Action: Move the plant pot two to three feet away from the intensely sunny window. Position the plant pot in a location where the incoming natural daylight is slightly filtered or casts a soft, blurry shadow rather than a harsh, sharply defined shadow.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The reddish-brown discoloration will fade away, and the leaves will slowly transition back to a normal greyish-green color, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.

### Pale Stretched and Bending Leaves

* Care need: low_light
* Visible signs: The new leaves emerging from the center of the rosette grow exceptionally long, narrow, and display a pale, washed-out light green color. The overly elongated leaves lack internal strength and bend sharply or crease horizontally under the weight of the leaf tissue instead of standing rigidly upright.
* Healthy contrast: Healthy new leaves grow thick, wide at the base, darkly colored, and maintain a strict, upright posture straight out of the center rosette.
* Not to be confused with: Pale stretched and bending leaves should not be confused with the overall shrinking caused by a water deficit. A water deficit causes all existing leaves to wrinkle and thin out simultaneously, whereas low light causes only the brand new leaves to stretch abnormally long, turn pale, and bend sharply in the middle.
* Likely cause: The Aloe vera is located in a deeply shaded corner of the room and is actively stretching weak new growth to find a stronger source of ambient light.
* Action: Relocate the plant pot to a much brighter room with a window that receives bright, indirect sunlight or gentle morning direct sunlight. Ensure the new location is bright enough for a human to easily read a printed book without turning on an electric light.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The currently bent and stretched leaves will remain permanently creased, but brand new leaves emerging from the soil will grow thick, compact, and strictly upright, typically within 4 weeks, at most 12 weeks.

### Black Water-Soaked Patches

* Care need: temperature_stress
* Visible signs: Large, dark black patches with a wet, mushy, collapsed appearance form abruptly on the fleshy leaves. The black mushy damage frequently appears on the specific side of the plant physically situated closest to a cold window pane or a drafty exterior door.
* Healthy contrast: A healthy Aloe vera features entirely green foliage that remains structurally firm, thick, and visually dry to the eye.
* Not to be confused with: Black water-soaked patches should not be confused with the reddish-brown discoloration caused by excess light. The reddish-brown discoloration from excess light leaves the plant tissue completely firm and visually dry, whereas temperature stress causes broad sections of the leaf interior to turn black, burst, and appear visibly wet.
* Likely cause: The Aloe vera has been exposed to freezing outdoor temperatures, a severe cold draft from an air conditioner, or direct physical contact with a freezing glass windowpane.
* Action: Move the plant pot to a warm, stable indoor environment far away from drafty exterior doors, cold windows, and active cooling vents. Prune away the visibly black and mushy leaf sections using a pair of clean household scissors.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The removed black sections cannot be healed, but the remaining healthy green leaf tissue will stop developing new mushy black patches, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.

### Small White Cotton-like Masses

* Care need: pest
* Visible signs: Tiny, fluffy white blobs that closely resemble small tufts of cotton appear wedged deeply down inside the tight central rosette where the new leaves emerge. The fluffy white blobs may also cluster in the tight crevices where the older outer leaves attach to the main base of the plant.
* Healthy contrast: The central rosette and the deep base crevices of a healthy Aloe vera are completely clean, smooth, and free of any white fuzz or sticky residue.
* Not to be confused with: Small white cotton-like masses should not be confused with the normal, flat white spots found on young leaves. The normal white spots on young leaves are completely flat and embedded directly inside the green leaf skin, whereas the pest masses are fluffy, raised, and sit on top of the leaf surfaces inside the crevices.
* Likely cause: Mealybugs have infested the plant and are actively hiding in the protected central crevices to feed on the internal plant sap.
* Action: Wipe the visible fluffy white masses out of the deep leaf crevices using a cotton swab heavily soaked in standard rubbing alcohol. Spray the central rosette of the plant lightly with a commercial insecticidal soap designed specifically for indoor succulents.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The visible white cotton-like masses will disappear immediately upon removal, and no new fluffy white patches will reappear in the central rosette, typically within 1 week, at most 3 weeks.

### Dark Sunken Circular Spots

* Care need: disease
* Visible signs: Distinct, sunken, circular craters with dark brown or black centers appear randomly scattered across the broad, flat upper surfaces of the leaves. The dark sunken craters range in size from tiny specks to large dry patches and do not wipe off when rubbed.
* Healthy contrast: Healthy Aloe vera leaves are completely smooth and free of any sunken craters or dark brown circular spots on the flat leaf faces.
* Not to be confused with: Dark sunken circular spots should not be confused with the normal pale teeth on the leaf margins. The normal pale teeth are raised spikes located exclusively on the extreme outer edge of the leaf, whereas the disease spots are sunken, dark circles occurring directly on the flat interior face of the leaf.
* Likely cause: A fungal leaf spot infection has developed due to excessive moisture sitting permanently on the leaf surfaces in a room with very poor air circulation.
* Action: Move the plant pot to an open area with much better room air circulation. Ensure that all future watering is applied strictly to the potting soil, entirely avoiding getting the fleshy green leaves wet.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The existing dark sunken circular spots will remain as permanent scars on the leaves, but no new spots will form on the remaining healthy foliage, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.
