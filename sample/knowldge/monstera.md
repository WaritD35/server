# Monstera (Monstera deliciosa) — visual care reference

## Healthy appearance

A healthy Monstera displays large, glossy, deep green leaves that stand outward on thick, sturdy stems. Mature leaves on a healthy Monstera feature deep natural splits and holes known botanically as fenestrations. The main green stems of a healthy Monstera appear thick, firm, and will naturally climb upward if provided with a vertical support structure. A beginner might mistake the long, brown, woody cords growing out of the stems for a disease or a fungal problem. The long, brown, woody cords are completely normal aerial roots that the Monstera uses to anchor itself to tree trunks in nature. A beginner might also mistake the solid, unbroken heart shape of newly formed or younger leaves for a sign of poor health. The solid, unbroken heart shape is entirely normal for young Monstera leaves, as the signature splits only develop as the plant matures and receives adequate sunlight over time. The visible surface of the potting soil inside the container typically appears dark and slightly textured without heavy white mineral crusts or excessive fluffy mold.

## Problems

### Widespread Yellowing of Lower Leaves

* Care need: overwatering
* Visible signs: Multiple older, lower leaves located nearest to the potting soil turn a solid, bright yellow color simultaneously. The yellow lower leaves often droop slightly downward and may eventually develop soft, dark brown patches directly in the center of the yellow areas.
* Healthy contrast: The lower, older leaves of a healthy Monstera maintain the exact same deep green color as the upper canopy foliage.
* Not to be confused with: Widespread yellowing of lower leaves should not be confused with the very slow yellowing of a single oldest leaf. The natural shedding of a single oldest leaf happens gradually over many weeks, whereas overwatering causes several lower leaves to turn bright yellow at the exact same time.
* Likely cause: The potting soil remains constantly soggy for extended periods, effectively cutting off oxygen to the plant roots.
* Action: Stop watering the Monstera immediately. Wait until the top surface of the potting soil looks significantly lighter in color and appears completely dry before providing any more water.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The currently bright yellow leaves will permanently die and fall off, but the rapid yellowing process will stop spreading to the remaining healthy green leaves, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.

### Severe Wilting and Limp Stems

* Care need: water_deficit
* Visible signs: The entire plant canopy droops significantly downward toward the floor. The thick green stems lose structural firmness and look slightly shriveled, causing the large, heavy leaves to hang limply instead of standing outward.
* Healthy contrast: The stems of a well-hydrated Monstera stand rigidly upright and outward, holding the large, heavy leaves firmly in a perky posture.
* Not to be confused with: Severe wilting and limp stems should not be confused with the natural downward growth of aerial roots. Aerial roots naturally grow straight downward from the nodes, whereas a water deficit causes the actual green leaf-supporting stems to collapse and droop.
* Likely cause: The potting soil inside the container has become completely bone dry throughout the entire root zone.
* Action: Pour room-temperature water evenly over the entire top surface of the soil until a steady stream flows directly out of the bottom drainage holes of the pot. Empty the saucer beneath the plant pot thirty minutes after the initial watering to prevent stagnant water accumulation.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The limp green stems will regain interior water pressure and physically lift the heavy leaves back to a normal upright posture, typically within 12 hours, at most 48 hours.

### Solid Green New Leaves Without Splits

* Care need: low_light
* Visible signs: The plant produces unusually small new leaves that completely lack the characteristic deep splits or internal holes. The main vines grow unusually long and thin, creating unusually large, bare gaps between each new solid green leaf.
* Healthy contrast: A mature, healthy Monstera consistently produces very large new leaves featuring multiple deep splits and prominent internal holes.
* Not to be confused with: Solid green new leaves without splits should not be confused with the naturally solid leaves of a very young juvenile plant. A very young juvenile plant naturally produces small solid leaves because of age, whereas low light causes an already mature, split-producing plant to suddenly revert to producing small, split-free leaves on stretched stems.
* Likely cause: The plant is located too far from a window and lacks the light energy required to produce large, complex fenestrated leaves.
* Action: Move the plant pot much closer to a window that provides bright, indirect sunlight. Ensure the Monstera sits in a room bright enough for a human to easily read a printed book without turning on an electric ceiling light.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The existing small, solid leaves will never develop splits later, but newly emerging leaves unrolling from the top of the vine will begin showing natural splits and holes again, typically within 4 weeks, at most 12 weeks.

### Dry Brown Crispy Leaf Edges

* Care need: low_humidity
* Visible signs: The outermost margins and the pointed tips of the large leaves turn dry, brown, and highly brittle. The brown crispy margins are frequently surrounded by a very thin yellow border that visibly separates the dead brown tissue from the healthy green leaf interior.
* Healthy contrast: The leaf margins of a healthy Monstera remain entirely green, smooth, and physically flexible right up to the very edge of the foliage.
* Not to be confused with: Dry brown crispy leaf edges should not be confused with the large, soft black patches caused by cold temperature damage. Cold temperature damage causes soft, wet-looking black patches anywhere on the flat leaf surface, whereas low humidity causes physically dry, brittle brown edges strictly along the outer perimeter.
* Likely cause: The ambient indoor air is excessively dry, often due to active indoor heating systems or air conditioning units.
* Action: Place a wide, shallow tray filled with pebbles and water directly beneath the plant pot. Ensure the bottom of the plant pot rests completely firmly on the dry pebbles and does not touch the liquid water in the tray.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The existing brown crispy edges will never turn green again, but brand new leaves will unfurl with completely smooth, green margins, typically within 3 weeks, at most 6 weeks.

### Pale Faded Leaves with Sunken Brown Scorch Marks

* Care need: excess_light
* Visible signs: The broad leaves located at the very top of the plant or physically closest to the window develop a washed-out, pale yellowish-green color. Large, dry, papery brown scorch marks appear directly in the center of the faded upper leaves directly facing the strongest light source.
* Healthy contrast: The uppermost leaves of a healthy Monstera display a deep, rich, uniform glossy green color across the entire top surface of the leaf canopy.
* Not to be confused with: Pale faded leaves with sunken brown scorch marks should not be confused with the bright yellowing caused by overwatering. The bright yellowing from overwatering affects the lowest leaves near the potting soil, whereas excess light physically bleaches and burns exclusively the top canopy leaves directly facing the sun.
* Likely cause: Direct, harsh afternoon sunlight is hitting the plant canopy and physically burning the delicate leaf tissue.
* Action: Relocate the plant pot several feet further away from the intensely sunny window. Position the plant pot in a location where the incoming natural daylight casts a soft, blurry shadow rather than a harsh, sharply defined shadow.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The burned papery brown patches and severely bleached leaves will not heal, but the plant will start producing newly emerging healthy dark green foliage without scorch marks, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.

### Fine White Webbing and Dusty Foliage

* Care need: pest
* Visible signs: Extremely fine, tiny white webbing stretches directly across the deep splits in the mature leaves or bridges the narrow gaps where the leaf stems connect to the main vine. The large leaves covered by the fine white webbing develop a dull, dusty appearance speckled with hundreds of tiny, pale yellow or white dots.
* Healthy contrast: The deep splits and main stems of a healthy Monstera remain completely free of any webbing and display a smooth, completely solid green upper surface.
* Not to be confused with: Fine white webbing and dusty foliage should not be confused with normal household dust settling on the large flat leaves. Normal household dust easily wipes away leaving a vibrant, solid green leaf underneath, whereas a pest infestation leaves permanent tiny pale stippling marks directly inside the plant tissue.
* Likely cause: Spider mites have infested the plant foliage and are actively piercing the leaf tissue to feed on the internal plant sap.
* Action: Wipe down all visible leaf surfaces and green stems with a damp cloth to physically remove the spider mite webbing. Spray the entire visible plant canopy thoroughly with a commercial insecticidal soap designed specifically for indoor houseplants.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The visible fine webbing will vanish immediately upon wiping, and no new webbing will appear across the leaf splits, typically within 1 week, at most 3 weeks.

### Black Water-Soaked Patches on Foliage

* Care need: temperature_stress
* Visible signs: Broad sections of the large leaves suddenly turn a very dark black color and take on a wet, mushy, collapsed appearance. The black mushy damage often appears abruptly on the specific side of the plant physically situated closest to a cold window pane or a drafty exterior door.
* Healthy contrast: A healthy Monstera features entirely green foliage that remains structurally firm and visually dry to the eye.
* Not to be confused with: Black water-soaked patches on foliage should not be confused with dry brown leaf edges caused by a lack of humidity. Dry brown edges are strictly confined to the perimeter of the leaf and feel dry to the eye, whereas temperature stress causes broad sections of the leaf interior to turn black and appear visibly wet or limp.
* Likely cause: The Monstera has been exposed to freezing temperatures, a severe cold draft, or direct physical contact with a freezing glass window pane.
* Action: Move the plant pot to a warm, stable indoor environment far away from drafty exterior doors, drafty windows, and active air conditioning vents. Prune away all the visibly black and mushy plant parts using a pair of clean household scissors.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The removed black sections cannot be restored, but the remaining healthy green vines and leaves will stop developing new mushy black patches, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.
