# Fiddle leaf fig (Ficus lyrata) — visual care reference

## Healthy appearance

A healthy Fiddle leaf fig displays large, distinctly violin-shaped leaves featuring a glossy, deep green color and prominent, lighter green veins. The leaves of a healthy Fiddle leaf fig stand firmly outward from the main trunk, either held perfectly horizontal or angled slightly upward toward the ceiling. A healthy plant features a stiff, upright woody trunk that easily supports the heavy weight of the large foliage canopy. A beginner might mistake the appearance of tiny reddish-brown dots completely covering a brand new, emerging leaf for a pest infestation. The tiny reddish-brown dots on a brand new leaf are a completely normal condition called edema caused by irregular water uptake, and the dots will naturally fade away entirely as the young leaf grows and hardens. A beginner might also mistake the slow yellowing and eventual dropping of a single oldest leaf at the very bottom of the trunk for a severe watering problem. The gradual loss of a single very old lower leaf is a natural part of the Fiddle leaf fig's aging process as the tree grows taller. The visible surface of the potting soil inside the container typically appears slightly textured and dark brown without heavy white mineral crusts or excessive fluffy gray mold.

## Problems

### Severe Drooping of the Entire Canopy

* Care need: water_deficit
* Visible signs: The large, heavy leaves across the entire plant lose structural rigidity and hang sharply downward parallel to the main trunk. The green stems connecting the leaves to the trunk look physically limp and slightly shriveled.
* Healthy contrast: The leaf stems of a well-hydrated Fiddle leaf fig hold the large leaves firmly horizontal or slightly upward, completely perpendicular to the main trunk.
* Not to be confused with: Severe drooping of the entire canopy should not be confused with the natural downward pointing of newly unfurled leaves. Newly unfurled leaves temporarily point downward while physically soft, whereas a water deficit causes mature, previously horizontal leaves to suddenly collapse and hang straight down.
* Likely cause: The potting soil inside the container has become completely bone dry throughout the entire root zone.
* Action: Pour room-temperature water evenly over the entire top surface of the potting soil until a steady stream of water exits the bottom drainage holes of the pot. Empty the saucer beneath the plant pot thirty minutes after the initial watering to prevent the pot from sitting in stagnant water.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The limp leaf stems will regain internal water pressure and physically lift the heavy drooping leaves back to a normal horizontal posture, typically within 12 hours, at most 48 hours.

### Large Dark Brown Patches on Lower Leaves

* Care need: overwatering
* Visible signs: Large, dark brown, visibly soft patches develop on the lowest and oldest leaves near the potting soil. The dark brown patches often start at the outer edge of the leaf and spread rapidly inward toward the center vein, frequently surrounded by a bright yellow halo.
* Healthy contrast: The lowest older leaves of a healthy Fiddle leaf fig maintain a solid, deep green color right to the very edge without any dark brown patches or yellow halos.
* Not to be confused with: Large dark brown patches on lower leaves should not be confused with dry crispy brown leaf edges caused by low humidity. Dry crispy brown edges caused by low humidity feel brittle and physically dry to the eye, whereas overwatering causes large, soft, dark brown patches that appear visibly wet or mushy.
* Likely cause: The potting soil remains constantly wet and soggy for extended periods, suffocating the roots and causing root rot.
* Action: Stop adding water to the Fiddle leaf fig immediately. Wait to water again until the top surface of the potting soil appears significantly lighter in color and looks completely dry.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The existing dark brown patches will permanently scar the lower leaves, but the rapid spreading of new brown patches will completely stop, typically within 1 week, at most 3 weeks.

### Sudden Dropping of Completely Green Leaves

* Care need: temperature_stress
* Visible signs: Large numbers of completely green, healthy-looking leaves suddenly detach from the trunk and fall directly onto the soil surface. The fallen green leaves show no obvious signs of yellowing, browning, or wilting prior to suddenly dropping off the tree.
* Healthy contrast: A healthy Fiddle leaf fig retains completely green, firm leaves tightly attached to the main woody trunk.
* Not to be confused with: The sudden dropping of completely green leaves should not be confused with the dropping of yellowed leaves caused by overwatering. Overwatering causes the leaves to change color entirely to bright yellow or brown before eventually falling, whereas temperature stress causes leaves to drop abruptly while the dropped leaves still look vibrant and completely green.
* Likely cause: The Fiddle leaf fig has been exposed to a sudden cold draft from an open exterior door, a drafty winter window, or a powerful air conditioning vent.
* Action: Move the plant pot to a stable indoor location far away from drafty exterior doors, cold windows, and active heating or cooling vents. Ensure the new plant location maintains a consistent, warm room temperature.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The fallen green leaves cannot be reattached to the trunk, but the sudden dropping of healthy green foliage will completely cease, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.

### Dry Crispy Brown Leaf Edges

* Care need: low_humidity
* Visible signs: The outermost margins and the pointed tips of the leaves turn physically dry, light brown, and highly brittle. The light brown crispy margins frequently crack or tear on their own, and the dry brown margins are not surrounded by a yellow halo.
* Healthy contrast: The leaf margins of a healthy Fiddle leaf fig remain entirely green, smooth, and physically flexible right up to the very edge of the foliage.
* Not to be confused with: Dry crispy brown leaf edges should not be confused with the large dark brown patches caused by overwatering. The dark brown patches from overwatering appear soft, wet, and spread deeply into the center of the leaf, whereas low humidity causes physically brittle, light brown edges strictly confined to the outer perimeter.
* Likely cause: The ambient indoor air surrounding the plant is excessively dry, often resulting from active indoor winter heating systems.
* Action: Place a wide, shallow tray filled with pebbles and water directly beneath the plant pot. Ensure the bottom of the plant pot rests firmly on the dry pebbles and does not directly touch the liquid water sitting in the tray.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The existing brown crispy edges will never turn green again, but brand new leaves will eventually unfurl with completely smooth, green margins, typically within 3 weeks, at most 6 weeks.

### Leggy Stems with Small Pale New Leaves

* Care need: low_light
* Visible signs: The main trunk grows unusually tall and thin, creating unusually large, bare vertical gaps between each new leaf. The newly emerging leaves on the stretched trunk appear significantly smaller, much paler green, and lack the deep violin shape compared to the older leaves lower down on the tree.
* Healthy contrast: A healthy Fiddle leaf fig produces a thick, sturdy trunk with large, deep green leaves clustered closely together.
* Not to be confused with: Leggy stems with small pale new leaves should not be confused with the severe drooping caused by a water deficit. A water deficit causes all existing leaves to hang limply straight downward, whereas low light causes the plant to grow upward but appear visibly stretched, thin, and sparse.
* Likely cause: The Fiddle leaf fig is positioned in a dark corner and lacks the adequate light energy required to produce large, robust foliage.
* Action: Relocate the plant pot much closer to a window that provides bright, indirect sunlight. Ensure the Fiddle leaf fig sits in a room bright enough for a human to easily read a printed book without turning on an electric ceiling light.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The currently stretched trunk gaps will remain permanently bare, but brand new leaves emerging from the top of the trunk will grow large, deeply green, and closely spaced, typically within 4 weeks, at most 12 weeks.

### Pale Bleached Leaves with Sunken Scorch Marks

* Care need: excess_light
* Visible signs: The broad leaves located at the very top of the plant canopy physically closest to a window develop a washed-out, pale yellowish-white color. Large, dry, papery brown scorch marks appear directly in the center of the faded upper leaves directly facing the strongest sunlight.
* Healthy contrast: The uppermost leaves of a healthy Fiddle leaf fig display a deep, rich, uniform glossy green color across the entire top surface of the leaf canopy.
* Not to be confused with: Pale bleached leaves with sunken scorch marks should not be confused with the dark brown patches caused by overwatering. The dark brown patches from overwatering primarily affect the lowest, oldest leaves near the dirt, whereas excess light physically bleaches and burns exclusively the top canopy leaves directly facing the sun.
* Likely cause: Direct, harsh afternoon sunlight is striking the plant canopy and physically burning the delicate leaf tissue.
* Action: Move the plant pot several feet further away from the intensely sunny window. Position the plant pot in a location where the incoming natural daylight casts a soft, blurry shadow rather than a harsh, sharply defined shadow.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The burned papery brown patches and severely bleached leaves will not heal, but the plant will start producing newly emerging healthy dark green foliage without scorch marks, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.

### Fine White Webbing and Dusty Stippled Foliage

* Care need: pest
* Visible signs: Extremely fine, tiny white webbing bridges the narrow gaps where the leaf stems connect to the main woody trunk. The large green leaves covered by the fine white webbing develop a dull, dusty appearance speckled with hundreds of tiny, pale yellow or white dots.
* Healthy contrast: The stems and main trunk of a healthy Fiddle leaf fig remain completely free of any webbing and display a smooth, completely solid green upper leaf surface.
* Not to be confused with: Fine white webbing and dusty stippled foliage should not be confused with normal household dust settling on the large flat leaves. Normal household dust easily wipes away leaving a vibrant, solid green leaf underneath, whereas a pest infestation leaves permanent tiny pale stippling marks directly inside the plant tissue.
* Likely cause: Spider mites have infested the plant foliage and are actively piercing the leaf tissue to feed on the internal plant sap.
* Action: Wipe down all visible leaf surfaces and green stems with a damp cloth to physically remove the spider mite webbing. Spray the entire visible plant canopy thoroughly with a commercial insecticidal soap designed specifically for indoor houseplants.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The visible fine webbing will vanish immediately upon wiping, and no new webbing will appear on the leaf stems, typically within 1 week, at most 3 weeks.
