# Snake plant (Dracaena trifasciata, syn. Sansevieria) — visual care reference

## Healthy appearance

A healthy Snake plant displays stiff, sword-shaped leaves that emerge directly from the soil surface and stand rigidly upright. The leaves of a healthy Snake plant feature a dark green background color crossed by horizontal bands of lighter green or silvery-gray. Many healthy Snake plant varieties also display a solid, bright yellow border running continuously along the entire outer edge of every leaf. A healthy plant grows in dense, tight clusters, with the stiff leaves coming together closely at the base near the soil line. A beginner might mistake the natural, slow outward leaning of the outermost older leaves for a sign of distress. The slight outward leaning of the outermost leaves is completely normal as the central plant cluster naturally expands and pushes the older foliage outward. A beginner might also worry about occasional horizontal cracks or dry, brown scars appearing on older leaves. Occasional horizontal cracking is a harmless physical blemish caused by past mechanical damage or temporary stress, not an ongoing disease. The visible surface of the potting soil usually appears dry, light-colored, and completely devoid of any green moss or white fungal growth.

## Problems

### Mushy, Collapsing Leaf Bases

* Care need: overwatering
* Visible signs: The leaves turn bright yellow or severely pale and become mushy directly at the soil line. The affected leaves completely collapse at the base and fall flat over the edge of the pot, even while the top halves of the collapsed leaves may still look green.
* Healthy contrast: The leaf bases of a healthy Snake plant are firm, completely green, and hold the entire heavy leaf perfectly vertical.
* Not to be confused with: Mushy, collapsing leaf bases should not be confused with the natural slight leaning of outermost leaves. The natural outward leaning involves rigid, firm leaves gently angling outward, whereas overwatering causes the base to rot away completely so the leaf flops over limply.
* Likely cause: The potting soil has remained constantly wet for too long, causing the fleshy leaf bases to rot.
* Action: Stop watering the plant immediately. Wait until the potting soil is visibly completely dry, and physically remove the collapsed leaves by cutting the fallen leaves off at the soil line with clean scissors.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The collapsed leaves will never stand up again, but the rotting process will stop spreading to the remaining upright leaves, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.

### Severely Wrinkled and Folding Leaves

* Care need: water_deficit
* Visible signs: Deep, vertical wrinkles run up and down the entire length of the green leaves. The usually flat or slightly curved leaves fold inward lengthwise, developing a sharp V-shape or U-shape cross-section as the foliage shrivels.
* Healthy contrast: The leaves of a well-hydrated Snake plant appear plump and smooth without any deep vertical wrinkles.
* Not to be confused with: Severely wrinkled and folding leaves should not be confused with the minor texture variations found on healthy foliage. Minor texture variations are shallow and do not change the shape of the leaf, whereas a water deficit causes the entire leaf body to physically fold inward and look severely dehydrated.
* Likely cause: The plant has not received water for several months, and the soil has become completely bone dry.
* Action: Pour room-temperature water evenly over the entire surface of the soil until a steady stream of water flows out of the drainage holes at the bottom of the pot. Empty the saucer underneath the pot thirty minutes after the initial watering to ensure the plant does not sit in standing water.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The vertical wrinkles will slowly plump up and smooth out, and the folded leaves will flatten open, typically within 1 week, at most 3 weeks.

### Tall, Thin, and Bending Leaves

* Care need: low_light
* Visible signs: New leaves grow exceptionally tall, pale, and much narrower than the older leaves on the plant. The excessively tall new leaves cannot support their own weight and bend or flop over loosely near the middle of the leaf blade.
* Healthy contrast: Healthy new leaves grow wide, dark green, and thick enough to stand completely upright without bending in the middle.
* Not to be confused with: Tall, thin, and bending leaves should not be confused with mushy, collapsing leaf bases caused by overwatering. Overwatering causes leaves to rot and detach exactly at the soil line, whereas low light causes weak leaves to bend in the middle while remaining firmly attached and dry at the base.
* Likely cause: The plant is located in a dark room and is stretching weakly toward a distant light source.
* Action: Relocate the plant pot to a brighter room with a window that receives bright, indirect sunlight. Ensure the new location is bright enough for a person to easily read a printed book without turning on an electric light.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The currently bent leaves will remain permanently bent, but brand new leaves emerging from the soil will grow wide, thick, and strictly upright, typically within 4 weeks, at most 12 weeks.

### White or Pale Bleached Patches

* Care need: excess_light
* Visible signs: Large, irregular patches on the side of the leaves facing the light source turn a faded white, pale yellow, or light brown. The discolored white patches appear dry and sometimes papery in texture on otherwise upright, firm leaves.
* Healthy contrast: The surfaces of healthy Snake plant leaves display strong, consistent green and gray patterns without any faded white, papery patches.
* Not to be confused with: White or pale bleached patches should not be confused with the natural silvery-gray cross-banding pattern of the species. The natural silvery-gray banding stretches horizontally across the entire leaf in regular repeating intervals, whereas bleached patches are irregular, faded spots localized only on the sun-facing side of the plant.
* Likely cause: Direct, harsh afternoon sunlight is striking the plant and physically scorching the leaf tissue.
* Action: Move the plant pot a few feet away from the intensely sunny window. Position the plant pot in a location where the incoming sunlight casts a soft, blurry shadow rather than a harsh, sharply defined shadow.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The bleached white patches will never turn green again, but the plant will stop developing new scorch marks on the healthy foliage, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.

### Black, Water-Soaked Leaf Spots

* Care need: temperature_stress
* Visible signs: Dark black, soft, and water-soaked looking patches appear suddenly on the upper halves of the leaves. The black patches often cause the top portion of the affected leaf to wilt and hang sharply downward.
* Healthy contrast: A healthy Snake plant leaf is entirely firm, dry, and maintains a consistent green and gray coloration right to the pointed tip.
* Not to be confused with: Black, water-soaked leaf spots should not be confused with dry, brown, healed scars from old physical damage. Dry brown scars are hard, stable, and never spread, whereas temperature stress causes black, wet-looking patches that expand quickly.
* Likely cause: The plant has been exposed to freezing temperatures or a severe cold draft from an open winter window.
* Action: Move the plant pot into a warm, stable indoor room far away from drafty windows and exterior doors. Use a pair of clean household scissors to cut off the black, mushy top sections of the affected leaves just below the damaged area.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The removed black sections cannot be healed, but the remaining healthy green leaf tissue will stop developing new black patches, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.

### Reddish-Brown Sunken Spots

* Care need: disease
* Visible signs: Distinct, sunken circular spots with reddish-brown centers and yellow halos appear randomly scattered across the flat surfaces of the leaves. The spots range in size from tiny specks to large dry craters.
* Healthy contrast: Healthy Snake plant leaves are completely smooth and free of any sunken craters or reddish-brown circular spots.
* Not to be confused with: Reddish-brown sunken spots should not be confused with the normal, solid yellow leaf margins found on some varieties. The normal yellow margins run smoothly and consistently along the very edge of the leaf, whereas the disease spots are irregular, reddish-brown circles surrounded by a thin yellow halo occurring anywhere on the flat leaf face.
* Likely cause: A fungal infection has taken hold due to poor air circulation and excessive moisture sitting directly on the leaf surfaces.
* Action: Move the plant pot to an open area with much better air circulation. Wipe the leaves completely dry with a towel, and physically cut away the most severely spotted leaves at the soil line using clean scissors.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The existing reddish-brown spots will remain permanently scarred, but no new spots will form on the remaining healthy foliage, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.

### White Cotton-like Masses in Crevices

* Care need: pest
* Visible signs: Small, fuzzy white masses that resemble tiny tufts of cotton appear wedged deeply into the tight central crevices where the leaves emerge together from the soil. The leaves immediately surrounding the white masses may look sticky or slightly shiny.
* Healthy contrast: The central base crevices of a healthy Snake plant are completely clean, dry, and free of any white fuzz or sticky residue.
* Not to be confused with: White cotton-like masses should not be confused with dry, white mineral crusts on the soil surface. Mineral crusts lay flat directly on the dirt, whereas the pest masses are fluffy and stick to the plant leaves themselves deep inside the central cluster.
* Likely cause: Mealybugs have infested the plant and are hiding in the protected bases to feed on the internal plant sap.
* Action: Wipe the visible fluffy white masses out of the leaf crevices using a cotton swab heavily dipped in standard rubbing alcohol. Spray the affected central areas of the plant lightly with a commercial insecticidal soap designed specifically for houseplants.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The visible white cotton-like masses will disappear immediately upon removal, and no new fluffy white patches will reappear in the base crevices, typically within 1 week, at most 3 weeks.
