# Calathea (Goeppertia) — visual care reference

## Healthy appearance

A healthy Calathea displays broad, richly patterned leaves held aloft on firm, upright stems. The leaves of a healthy Calathea feature intricate designs of dark green, light green, pink, or white on the upper surface. A healthy plant exhibits a daily movement cycle where the foliage lays relatively flat during the day and stands almost straight up at night. A beginner might mistake the nightly upright folding of the leaves for a sign of severe wilting or physical stress. The nightly upright folding of the leaves is a completely normal botanical behavior called nyctinasty. A beginner might also mistake a newly emerging leaf for a deformed or dying leaf. A newly emerging leaf naturally grows tightly rolled up like a green vertical cigar before slowly unfurling completely flat over several days. The visible surface of the potting soil inside the container generally appears dark brown and slightly textured, lacking any heavy white crusts or thick layers of gray mold.

## Problems

### Brown Crispy Edges

* Care need: low_humidity
* Visible signs: The outermost margins and the pointed tips of the leaves turn dry, brown, and brittle. The dry brown margins often feature a very thin yellow border separating the dead brown tissue from the healthy green interior tissue.
* Healthy contrast: The leaf margins of a healthy Calathea remain completely smooth, vibrant, and physically flexible right up to the very edge of the leaf.
* Not to be confused with: Brown crispy edges should not be confused with the mushy yellowing caused by overwatering. The mushy yellowing from overwatering affects the entire leaf body and looks soft, whereas low humidity causes exclusively the edges to become physically dry and brittle while the leaf center remains green.
* Likely cause: The ambient indoor air is excessively dry, or the plant is reacting to harsh mineral buildups present in unfiltered tap water.
* Action: Place a wide, shallow tray filled with pebbles and water directly underneath the plant pot. Ensure the bottom of the plant pot rests completely on top of the dry pebbles and does not touch the liquid water in the tray. Switch to using distilled water or collected rainwater for all future watering sessions.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The existing brown crispy edges will never heal, but newly emerging leaves will unfurl without any brown dry edges, typically within 3 weeks, at most 6 weeks.

### Inward Curling Leaves

* Care need: water_deficit
* Visible signs: The large flat leaves tightly roll their left and right edges inward toward the center vein to form a narrow hollow tube. The stems supporting the rolled leaves lose structural rigidity and droop visibly downward toward the surface of the potting soil.
* Healthy contrast: The leaves of a well-hydrated Calathea lay fully open and completely flat during the daytime hours.
* Not to be confused with: Inward curling leaves should not be confused with the natural emergence of a brand new leaf. A natural brand new leaf emerges as a single tight vertical spike directly from the center of the plant, whereas a water deficit causes previously mature, flat leaves to suddenly roll up into tubes.
* Likely cause: The potting soil inside the container has become completely dry throughout the entire root zone.
* Action: Pour room-temperature water evenly over the entire top surface of the potting soil until a steady stream of water exits the bottom drainage holes of the pot. Empty the saucer beneath the plant pot thirty minutes after the initial watering to prevent stagnant water accumulation around the base.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The rolled leaves will unroll flat and the drooping stems will stand upright again, typically within 4 hours, at most 24 hours.

### Widespread Yellowing of Lower Leaves

* Care need: overwatering
* Visible signs: Multiple older leaves located nearest to the potting soil turn a bright, solid yellow color at exactly the same time. The stems attached to the bright yellow leaves often look soft, turn dark brown, and collapse directly onto the rim of the pot.
* Healthy contrast: The lowest older leaves of a healthy Calathea maintain the same intricate color patterns and upright posture as the upper canopy leaves.
* Not to be confused with: Widespread yellowing of lower leaves should not be confused with the natural fading of a single oldest leaf. The natural fading of a single oldest leaf happens extremely slowly over many months, whereas overwatering causes several lower leaves to abruptly turn bright yellow simultaneously.
* Likely cause: The potting soil remains constantly wet and soggy for extended periods, completely suffocating the plant roots.
* Action: Stop adding any water to the plant pot immediately. Wait until the top surface of the potting soil appears visibly lighter in color and looks completely dry before adding any more water.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The currently bright yellow leaves will permanently die, but the rapid yellowing process will stop spreading to the remaining healthy leaves, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.

### Faded and Bleached Leaf Patterns

* Care need: excess_light
* Visible signs: The intricate, contrasting patterns on the uppermost leaves fade into a washed-out, uniform pale yellowish-green. Large, dry, papery brown scorch marks frequently appear directly in the center of the faded uppermost leaves.
* Healthy contrast: The uppermost leaves of a healthy Calathea display highly contrasting, sharply defined patterns featuring deep green, bright white, or vivid pink colors.
* Not to be confused with: Faded and bleached leaf patterns should not be confused with an overall dullness caused by spider mites. Spider mites cause a dusty appearance with tiny distinct specks across all leaves, whereas excess light physically bleaches out the broad color patches entirely on the top canopy directly facing the window.
* Likely cause: Direct, intense sunlight is hitting the plant canopy and physically burning the delicate leaf tissue.
* Action: Move the plant pot several feet away from the intensely sunny window. Position the plant pot in a location where the incoming natural daylight casts a soft, blurry shadow rather than a harsh, sharply defined shadow.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The burned papery patches and severely bleached leaves will not regain distinct patterns, but the plant will start producing newly patterned, deeply colored leaves, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.

### Fine Webbing and Dusty Foliage

* Care need: pest
* Visible signs: Extremely fine white webbing bridges the narrow gaps where the leaf stems connect to the main base of the plant. The broad leaves covered by the fine white webbing develop a dull, dusty appearance scattered with hundreds of tiny, pale yellow specks.
* Healthy contrast: The stems and leaves of a healthy Calathea remain completely free of any webbing and display a smooth, completely clean upper surface without tiny yellow specks.
* Not to be confused with: Fine webbing and dusty foliage should not be confused with normal household dust collecting on the broad leaves. Normal household dust easily wipes away leaving a vibrant leaf underneath, whereas a pest infestation leaves permanent pale stippling marks directly inside the leaf tissue.
* Likely cause: Spider mites have heavily infested the plant foliage and are actively piercing the leaf tissue to feed on the internal plant sap.
* Action: Wipe down all visible leaf surfaces and stems with a damp cloth to physically remove the spider mite webbing. Spray the entire visible plant canopy thoroughly with a commercial insecticidal soap designed specifically for indoor houseplants.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The visible fine webbing will vanish immediately upon wiping, and no new webbing will appear on the plant stems, typically within 1 week, at most 3 weeks.

### Leggy Stems with Small Dull Leaves

* Care need: low_light
* Visible signs: The plant produces unusually long, thin green stems that stretch far outward from the center of the pot. The new leaves growing on the unusually long stems appear significantly smaller than the older leaves and lack clearly defined contrasting color patterns.
* Healthy contrast: A healthy Calathea produces a dense, compact cluster of robust stems topped with large, vividly patterned leaves.
* Not to be confused with: Leggy stems with small dull leaves should not be confused with the drooping stems caused by a water deficit. Drooping stems from a water deficit point straight downward due to limpness, whereas leggy stems grow upward but appear stretched, thin, and visibly sparse.
* Likely cause: The plant is positioned in a deeply shaded corner and is actively stretching its stems to find a stronger source of ambient light.
* Action: Move the plant pot significantly closer to a window that provides bright, indirect sunlight. Ensure the plant pot is placed in a room bright enough for a human to easily read a printed book without turning on an electric ceiling light.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The currently stretched stems will remain permanently long, but brand new stems emerging from the soil will grow thicker and much more compact, typically within 4 weeks, at most 8 weeks.

### Dark Water-Soaked Spots

* Care need: disease
* Visible signs: Circular, dark brown or black spots with a wet, mushy appearance form randomly across the broad flat surfaces of the leaves. The wet-looking dark spots frequently feature a distinct, bright yellow ring directly surrounding the dark center.
* Healthy contrast: Healthy Calathea leaves are completely smooth and free of any wet-looking craters or dark brown circular spots.
* Not to be confused with: Dark water-soaked spots should not be confused with dry brown crispy edges caused by low humidity. Dry brown crispy edges are strictly confined to the outermost perimeter of the leaf and feel brittle, whereas disease spots appear as wet circles randomly located anywhere on the interior flat face of the leaf.
* Likely cause: A bacterial or fungal leaf spot infection has taken hold due to poor air circulation and excessive moisture sitting permanently on the leaf surfaces.
* Action: Move the plant pot to an open area with much better room air circulation. Physically cut away the most severely spotted leaves near the soil line using clean household scissors to prevent the spread of the infection.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The existing dark water-soaked spots will remain permanently scarred, but no new spots will form on the remaining healthy green foliage, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.
