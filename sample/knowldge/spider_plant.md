# Spider plant (Chlorophytum comosum) — visual care reference

## Healthy appearance

A healthy Spider plant features a dense, central rosette of long, slender leaves that naturally arch outward and downward over the rim of the pot. The foliage of a healthy Spider plant displays either a solid vibrant green color or a variegated pattern consisting of distinct green and crisp white or cream longitudinal stripes. A healthy Spider plant frequently produces long, stiff, pale yellow or green stems that cascade down and carry small white star-shaped flowers alongside miniature plantlets. A beginner might mistake the miniature plantlets hanging from the long stems for a bizarre mutation or a sign of the plant falling apart. The hanging miniature plantlets, also known as spiderettes, are a completely normal and healthy method of spider plant reproduction. A beginner might also mistake the natural folding or creasing at the very base of the long leaves for a structural defect. The natural V-shaped crease at the base of the leaf is completely normal and provides necessary structural support for the long, arching leaf blade. The visible surface of the potting soil inside the container typically appears dark brown and slightly textured, lacking any heavy white mineral crusts or thick gray mold.

## Problems

### Brown Crispy Leaf Tips

* Care need: low_humidity
* Visible signs: The sharply pointed tips of the long slender leaves turn dry, light brown, and highly brittle. The dry brown discoloration sometimes extends up to an inch down the leaf blade and appears distinctly physically dry to the naked eye.
* Healthy contrast: The leaf tips of a healthy Spider plant taper perfectly to a sharp point and remain entirely green or cleanly variegated right to the very end.
* Not to be confused with: Brown crispy leaf tips should not be confused with the mushy yellowing caused by overwatering. The mushy yellowing from overwatering affects the entire base of the leaf near the soil and looks visibly wet, whereas brown crispy tips exclusively affect the outermost floating points and look entirely physically dry.
* Likely cause: The ambient indoor air is excessively dry, or the plant is reacting negatively to harsh fluoride and chlorine chemicals found in standard municipal tap water.
* Action: Trim the dead brown tips off the leaves using clean household scissors, following the natural angled shape of the original leaf tip. Switch to using distilled water, collected rainwater, or filtered water for all future watering sessions.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The existing brown crispy leaf tips will never heal, but newly emerging leaves from the center of the plant will grow entirely without brown tips, typically within 3 weeks, at most 6 weeks.

### Pale Greyish-Green Limp Foliage

* Care need: water_deficit
* Visible signs: The normally vibrant green foliage fades to a dull, washed-out greyish-green color across the entire plant. The long leaves lose internal stiffness, causing the entire plant canopy to look dramatically deflated, wilted, and limp.
* Healthy contrast: The leaves of a well-hydrated Spider plant display a vibrant, rich color and arch outward with a strong, rigid, upright posture from the center rosette.
* Not to be confused with: Pale greyish-green limp foliage should not be confused with the bright yellow collapsing leaves caused by overwatering. Overwatering causes specific outer leaves to rot at the base and turn brightly yellow, whereas a water deficit causes the entire plant canopy to simultaneously turn a dull greyish-green and droop uniformly without rotting.
* Likely cause: The potting soil inside the container has become completely bone dry throughout the entire root zone, fully depleting the plant's internal water reserves.
* Action: Pour room-temperature water evenly over the entire top surface of the potting soil until a steady stream of water exits the bottom drainage holes of the pot. Empty the saucer beneath the plant pot thirty minutes after the initial watering to prevent the pot from sitting in stagnant water.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The dull limp leaves will regain internal water pressure, restore the vibrant green color, and physically lift back to a normal arching posture, typically within 12 hours, at most 48 hours.

### Mushy Yellowing at the Base

* Care need: overwatering
* Visible signs: The older leaves located at the outer bottom edge of the plant rosette turn a bright, sickly yellow and become visibly translucent or mushy directly at the soil line. The mushy yellow leaves completely lose structural strength and collapse flat against the rim of the pot or fall directly onto the potting soil.
* Healthy contrast: The outer bottom leaves of a healthy Spider plant maintain firm structural integrity at the soil line and easily support the arching weight of the long foliage.
* Not to be confused with: Mushy yellowing at the base should not be confused with the gradual fading of a single very old leaf. The natural gradual fading of a single old leaf happens slowly without becoming mushy at the base, whereas overwatering causes several leaves to simultaneously rot, turn intensely yellow, and collapse at the soil line.
* Likely cause: The potting soil remains constantly wet and soggy for extended periods, suffocating the fleshy tuberous plant roots and causing the base to rot.
* Action: Stop adding water to the Spider plant immediately. Wait to water again until the visible top surface of the potting soil appears significantly lighter in color and looks completely dry to the eye.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The collapsed yellow leaves will permanently die and fall off, but the rapid rotting process will stop spreading to the remaining healthy central leaves, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.

### Faded and Bleached Foliage

* Care need: excess_light
* Visible signs: The broad sections of the leaves directly facing the nearest window develop a washed-out, completely faded, pale yellowish-white color. The white stripes on variegated varieties may become so unnaturally wide that the green margins almost disappear entirely, and dry papery brown scorch marks may appear directly in the center of the faded areas.
* Healthy contrast: The leaves of a healthy variegated Spider plant display highly contrasting, sharply defined green and white patterns without any washed-out, faded patches.
* Not to be confused with: Faded and bleached foliage should not be confused with the overall dull greyish-green color caused by a water deficit. A water deficit affects the entire plant uniformly and causes severe drooping, whereas excess light physically bleaches exclusively the top side of the plant directly facing the intense sun while the leaves remain upright.
* Likely cause: Direct, harsh afternoon sunlight is hitting the plant foliage and physically bleaching or burning the delicate leaf tissue.
* Action: Move the plant pot several feet away from the intensely sunny window. Position the plant pot in a location where the incoming natural daylight casts a soft, blurry shadow rather than a harsh, sharply defined shadow.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The severely bleached leaves and burned brown patches will never regain normal coloring, but newly emerging leaves from the center rosette will display rich, healthy contrast, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.

### Weak Folded Leaves and No Plantlets

* Care need: low_light
* Visible signs: The new leaves emerging from the center of the plant grow unusually long, extremely narrow, and persistently fold entirely in half lengthwise. The plant stops producing any new pale yellow cascading stems, and absolutely no miniature plantlets form on the plant over a period of several months.
* Healthy contrast: A healthy Spider plant produces robust, wide leaves that remain mostly flat and readily pushes out long stems carrying miniature plantlets.
* Not to be confused with: Weak folded leaves and no plantlets should not be confused with a juvenile plant's natural lack of plantlets. A very young plant naturally lacks plantlets until reaching maturity, whereas low light causes a mature plant to suddenly stop reproducing and begin growing unusually narrow, weak, folded leaves.
* Likely cause: The Spider plant is positioned in a room that is much too dark and lacks the light energy required to produce strong, wide leaves and new offspring.
* Action: Relocate the plant pot much closer to a window that provides bright, indirect sunlight. Ensure the Spider plant sits in a room bright enough for a human to easily read a printed book without turning on an electric ceiling light.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The currently narrow, folded leaves will remain permanently thin, but brand new leaves emerging from the center will grow wide and robust, typically within 4 weeks, at most 8 weeks.

### Small Brown Oval Bumps on Leaves

* Care need: pest
* Visible signs: Small, raised, oval-shaped brown bumps appear firmly attached to the main green leaf blades and along the central leaf veins. The leaves directly below the brown oval bumps develop a highly reflective, sticky residue that makes the foliage look wet or heavily glossed.
* Healthy contrast: The leaves of a healthy Spider plant are completely smooth, visually dry, and entirely free of any raised brown bumps or sticky, shiny residue.
* Not to be confused with: Small brown oval bumps on leaves should not be confused with the tiny brown dried tips of the leaves. Tiny brown dried tips appear only at the extreme terminal points of the leaves and consist of flat dead tissue, whereas the pest bumps appear as distinct, raised 3D ovals clustered along the middle of the leaf blades.
* Likely cause: Scale insects have heavily infested the Spider plant and have physically attached themselves to the foliage to feed on the internal plant sap.
* Action: Wipe the visible brown oval bumps off the leaves using a cotton swab heavily soaked in standard rubbing alcohol. Spray the entire visible plant canopy thoroughly with a commercial horticultural oil or an insecticidal soap designed specifically for indoor houseplants.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The sticky shiny residue will disappear, and no new raised brown bumps will appear on the foliage, typically within 1 week, at most 3 weeks.

### Overall Pale Yellowing of Entire Plant

* Care need: nutrient_deficiency
* Visible signs: The entire plant canopy, including both the oldest outer leaves and the newest inner leaves, uniformly fades to a sickly, pale yellowish-green color. The overall pale Spider plant produces very slow growth and looks generally weak despite receiving appropriate light and water.
* Healthy contrast: A well-fed Spider plant displays a deep, rich green background color across the entire plant rosette and consistently pushes out fresh, vibrant new leaves from the center.
* Not to be confused with: Overall pale yellowing of the entire plant should not be confused with overwatering. Overwatering causes a stark, bright yellow color specifically on the lower outer leaves while the inner leaves stay green, whereas a nutrient deficiency causes the entire plant to simultaneously turn a dull, pale yellowish-green.
* Likely cause: The potting soil has been completely depleted of essential plant nutrients after housing the fast-growing Spider plant for several years without fresh fertilizer applications.
* Action: Apply a standard liquid houseplant fertilizer to the potting soil during the very next routine watering session. Ensure the liquid fertilizer is mixed with water exactly according to the package instructions to avoid root burn.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The overall pale yellowish-green foliage will gradually darken back into a healthy, vibrant green color, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.
