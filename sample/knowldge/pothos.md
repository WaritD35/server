# Pothos (Epipremnum aureum) — visual care reference

## Healthy appearance

A healthy Pothos displays smooth, waxy, heart-shaped leaves that grow along flexible, trailing vines. The foliage of a healthy Pothos exhibits a vibrant green base color, often marked with striking yellow, white, or cream marbling known as variegation. A healthy plant features vines that feel firm and look plump, naturally cascading downward over the sides of the pot or climbing upward if provided with a support structure. The leaves on a thriving Pothos stand outward from the main vine on stiff, independently supportive leaf stems. A beginner might mistake the small, brown, woody bumps growing along the main vines for a disease or a pest infestation. The small, brown, woody bumps are completely normal aerial roots that the Pothos uses to cling to surfaces in the wild. A beginner might also mistake the yellowing and dropping of a single oldest leaf near the potting soil for a watering problem. The occasional shedding of a single old leaf near the base of the plant is a natural part of the Pothos aging process. The visible surface of the potting soil inside the container generally appears dark brown and slightly textured, lacking any heavy white crusts or thick layers of gray mold.

## Problems

### Severe Wilting and Limp Foliage

* Care need: water_deficit
* Visible signs: The entire foliage canopy droops noticeably, with the individual heart-shaped leaves hanging completely flat and limp. The normally stiff leaf stems bend sharply downward toward the floor, giving the entire Pothos a deflated, collapsed appearance.
* Healthy contrast: The leaf stems of a well-hydrated Pothos stand rigidly outward from the main vine, holding the firm leaves in a perky, slightly upward-angled position.
* Not to be confused with: Severe wilting should not be confused with the natural downward trailing habit of the main Pothos vines. The natural downward trailing habit affects only the direction the main vine grows, whereas a water deficit causes the individual leaves and small leaf stems to become visibly soft and limp.
* Likely cause: The potting soil inside the container has become completely bone dry.
* Action: Pour room-temperature water evenly over the entire soil surface until a steady stream of water exits the bottom drainage holes of the pot. Empty the saucer beneath the plant pot thirty minutes after the initial watering to prevent the pot from sitting in stagnant water.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The soft leaf stems will regain water pressure and physically lift the drooping leaves back to an upright posture, typically within 4 hours, at most 24 hours.

### Widespread Yellowing of Base Leaves

* Care need: overwatering
* Visible signs: Multiple leaves located closest to the potting soil turn a bright, solid yellow color simultaneously. The bright yellow base leaves often look slightly soft and may easily detach from the main vine to fall directly onto the soil surface.
* Healthy contrast: The oldest leaves near the base of a healthy Pothos retain the same deep green color and firm texture as the newer leaves at the ends of the vines.
* Not to be confused with: Widespread yellowing of base leaves should not be confused with the natural yellow or white variegation patterns found on healthy Pothos leaves. Natural variegation appears as streaks or marbled patches on otherwise green leaves, whereas overwatering causes the entire leaf surface to become completely solid yellow.
* Likely cause: The potting soil remains constantly wet for extended periods, suffocating the plant roots by blocking oxygen airflow.
* Action: Stop adding water to the Pothos immediately. Wait to water again until the visible top surface of the potting soil appears significantly lighter in color and looks completely dry.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The currently bright yellow leaves will permanently fall off the plant, but the yellowing process will stop spreading to the remaining green leaves, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.

### Complete Loss of Variegation

* Care need: low_light
* Visible signs: The newly emerging leaves at the tips of the vines grow in as a completely solid green color without any yellow, white, or cream streaks. The main trailing vines also develop abnormally long, bare gaps between each new solid green leaf.
* Healthy contrast: The newest leaves on a healthy variegated Pothos display bright, highly visible yellow or white marbling mixed in with the green base color.
* Not to be confused with: A complete loss of variegation should not be confused with the natural appearance of specific solid-green Pothos varieties like the Jade Pothos. A solid-green Jade Pothos naturally produces only green leaves, whereas a loss of variegation occurs when a previously marbled plant suddenly starts producing purely solid green new growth.
* Likely cause: The Pothos is placed in a corner that is too dark, forcing the plant to produce purely green leaves to maximize photosynthesis.
* Action: Relocate the plant pot closer to a window that provides bright, indirect sunlight. Ensure the Pothos sits in a room that is bright enough for a person to easily read a printed book without turning on an electrical ceiling light.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The solid green leaves will remain solid green forever, but newly sprouting leaves at the ends of the vines will begin showing yellow or white marbling again, typically within 3 weeks, at most 8 weeks.

### Pale Bleached Leaves with Crispy Brown Patches

* Care need: excess_light
* Visible signs: The leaves located at the very top of the plant or closest to the window develop a faded, washed-out, pale yellowish-white color. The faded pale leaves frequently develop large, dry, crispy brown scorch marks directly in the center of the leaf tissue.
* Healthy contrast: The uppermost leaves of a healthy Pothos maintain a rich, glossy green base color without any dry brown patches in the center of the foliage.
* Not to be confused with: Pale bleached leaves with crispy brown patches should not be confused with the yellowing caused by overwatering. The yellowing from overwatering affects the lowest leaves near the soil, whereas bleached scorch marks appear exclusively on the top canopy leaves directly facing the sun.
* Likely cause: Direct, harsh sunlight is hitting the Pothos and physically burning the delicate leaf tissue.
* Action: Move the plant pot several feet away from the intensely sunny window. Position the plant pot in a location where the incoming light casts a soft, blurry shadow rather than a harsh, sharply defined shadow.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The burned brown patches and severely bleached leaves will not heal, but the plant will start producing healthy glossy green foliage without scorch marks, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.

### Blackened and Mushy Foliage

* Care need: temperature_stress
* Visible signs: Large sections of the leaves and vines turn a very dark black color and take on a wet, mushy, collapsed appearance. The black, mushy damage often appears abruptly on the side of the plant physically situated closest to a window pane or an exterior door.
* Healthy contrast: A healthy Pothos features entirely green or marbled foliage that remains structurally firm and dry to the eye.
* Not to be confused with: Blackened and mushy foliage should not be confused with dry brown leaf tips caused by a lack of humidity. Dry brown tips are light brown and brittle at the very ends of the leaves, whereas temperature stress causes broad sections of the leaf to turn black and appear visibly wet or limp.
* Likely cause: The Pothos has been exposed to freezing temperatures, a severe cold draft, or direct physical contact with a freezing window pane.
* Action: Move the plant pot to a stable indoor environment far away from drafty exterior doors, drafty windows, and active air conditioning vents. Prune away all the visibly black and mushy plant parts using a pair of clean household scissors.
* Urgency: this week
* Expected recovery: The removed black sections cannot be restored, but the remaining healthy green vines will stop developing new mushy black patches, typically within 1 week, at most 2 weeks.

### Small White Cotton-Like Masses on Vines

* Care need: pest
* Visible signs: Tiny, fluffy white blobs that closely resemble small pieces of cotton candy appear wedged into the tight crevices where the leaf stems attach to the main vine. The leaves immediately below the fluffy white masses may look uncharacteristically shiny and sticky.
* Healthy contrast: The crevices where the Pothos leaf stems meet the main vine are completely clean and smooth without any white fuzz or sticky residue.
* Not to be confused with: Small white cotton-like masses should not be confused with the Pothos plant's natural aerial roots. Natural aerial roots are hard, brown, woody nubs, whereas the pest masses are soft, bright white, and highly fuzzy.
* Likely cause: Mealybugs have infested the Pothos and are actively hiding in the stem crevices to feed on the plant sap.
* Action: Wipe all visible fluffy white masses off the vines and leaves using a cotton swab heavily soaked in standard rubbing alcohol. Spray the entire visible Pothos foliage and vines with a commercial insecticidal soap designed specifically for indoor houseplants.
* Urgency: today
* Expected recovery: The sticky shiny residue will disappear, and no new fluffy white cotton-like masses will appear in the vine crevices, typically within 1 week, at most 3 weeks.

### Overall Pale Yellowing of the Entire Plant Canopy

* Care need: nutrient_deficiency
* Visible signs: The entire Pothos plant, including both the oldest leaves at the base and the newest leaves on the vines, uniformly fades to a sickly, pale yellowish-green color. The overall pale Pothos produces unusually tiny new leaves and shows almost no new vine growth over a period of several months.
* Healthy contrast: A well-fed Pothos displays a deep, rich green background color across the entire plant canopy and consistently produces large, robust new leaves.
* Not to be confused with: Overall pale yellowing of the entire plant canopy should not be confused with overwatering. Overwatering causes a stark, bright yellow color specifically on the lower leaves while the upper leaves stay green, whereas a nutrient deficiency causes the entire plant to simultaneously turn a dull, pale yellowish-green.
* Likely cause: The potting soil has been completely depleted of essential plant nutrients after housing the Pothos for several years without fresh fertilizer.
* Action: Apply a standard liquid houseplant fertilizer to the potting soil during the very next routine watering session. Ensure the liquid fertilizer is mixed with water exactly according to the package instructions to avoid root burn.
* Urgency: this month
* Expected recovery: The overall pale yellowish-green foliage will gradually darken back into a healthy, vibrant green color, typically within 2 weeks, at most 4 weeks.
